Reviewing student theses, papers & slides with NotebookLM

Salam sejahtera dan selamat pagi, rakan-rakan sekalian! 👋 If your "to-review" pile is now tall enough to need its own parking space, this one's for you. This week we hand the first read of a student thesis, paper, or slide deck to NotebookLM — Google's tool that answers only from the document you upload and cites the exact page for every point. Think of it as a tireless teaching assistant who has actually read the whole thing, never sulks, and never once asks for a teh tarik break. 🤖☕ You stay the expert; it just clears the runway.

🧪 Prompt Minggu Ini  /  This Week's Prompt

Built on the RACE frame (Role · Action · Context · Expectation) + a critical-thinking checklist. Copy it, fill the [BRACKETS], paste into NotebookLM.
ROLE: You are an experienced thesis examiner and journal peer reviewer in [FIELD], known for rigorous but constructive feedback.

CONTEXT: The uploaded source is a student's [thesis / paper / presentation]. Base every comment ONLY on this document and cite the exact section, page, or slide for each point.

ACTION: Produce a structured review:
  1. One paragraph summarising the central argument, in your own words.
  2. Strengths (3–5, each with a citation).
  3. Critical evaluation — check each:
    (a) Is the research question & conclusion clear?
    (b) Are reasons & evidence sufficient and relevant?
    (c) What key assumptions go unstated?
    (d) Are terms defined and used consistently?
    (e) Any statistical/causal weakness (correlation vs. causation, small n, missing data)?
    (f) What significant information is omitted?
    (g) Are alternative conclusions possible?
  4. Priority fixes ranked High / Medium / Low.
  5. Three questions I should ask the student.

EXPECTATION: Specific, kind, plain language. No vague praise. If a point can't be verified from the source, say so rather than guessing.

A question bank from your own notes — with Gemini

Salam sejahtera dan selamat pagi, rakan-rakan sekalian! 👋 It's assessment season, and the blank question paper is staring back at you — again. Writing a dozen fair, well-aligned questions from scratch (with plausible distractors, no less) is the kind of task that quietly swallows an evening and a pot of kopi. 😅 This week we hand Gemini your lecture notes and let it draft a whole question bank — mapped to your learning outcomes and Bloom's levels, complete with an answer key. You stay the examiner-in-chief; Gemini just fills the first draft so you can spend your time sharpening, not staring.

🧪 Prompt Minggu Ini  /  This Week's Prompt

Technique this week: few-shot + specification prompting — show one model question in the exact format you want, then specify the mix (how many, which Bloom's levels, which outcomes). Paste this into Gemini with your notes.
HERE IS ONE EXAMPLE of the exact format I want:

S1 (Aras Bloom: Aplikasi | CLO2)
Which normalisation step removes partial dependencies on a composite key?
  A. 1NF   B. 2NF ✔   C. 3NF   D. BCNF
Rasional: 2NF removes partial dependencies; distractors are the adjacent normal forms students confuse.

NOW GENERATE A QUESTION BANK from my material below, in EXACTLY that format:
  • 8 MCQ + 4 short-answer questions.
  • Bloom's spread: 3 Ingatan/Kefahaman, 5 Aplikasi/Analisis, 4 Penilaian/Cipta.
  • Map every item to one of my CLOs; flag any CLO not covered.
  • Make MCQ distractors plausible, each with a one-line rasional.
  • Put a full answer key at the very end.

MY MATERIAL & OUTCOMES:

Stop re-prompting — build a reusable Gem

Salam sejahtera dan selamat pagi, rakan-rakan sekalian! 👋 Notice something about the last few issues? Each time, we typed out a careful prompt… and then next week, typed it out again. 😅 This week we break the loop. A Gem is a custom AI assistant you set up once — you save the instructions, give it a name, and from then on it's a single click in your Gemini sidebar. Better still, you can share it with the whole team, so everyone drafts the same way without re-explaining anything. This is the shift from clever prompting to renewed workflow: less re-typing, more actual work.

⚙️
Set up once
Reuse it forever
🤝
Team-wide
Everyone drafts the same way
🖱️
One click
Straight from the sidebar
🧠 Arahan Gem  /  The Gem's Instructions

This week the "prompt" becomes a saved instruction set — you paste it into the Gem builder once. Here's a worked example: a Gem that replies to student emails.
NAME: Penjawab E-mel Pelajar (Student Email Responder)

ROLE: You help me reply to student emails quickly, warmly, and professionally.

WHAT TO DO: When I paste a student's email, draft a reply in 4 sentences — acknowledge the question, answer it clearly, give one concrete next step, and close kindly. Approachable, not stiff.

RULES:
  • Match the language of the student's email (BM or English).
  • Never invent facts — dates, marks, policies. If unknown, tell me to fill it in.
  • If it's about grades or a sensitive matter, flag it and keep the tone extra careful.

DEFAULT: If I paste an email with no other instruction, produce the reply straight away.

↳ Swap these instructions for any task you repeat — Kertas Kerja, Bank Soalan, minit — and you've got that Gem.

A point-by-point response to reviewers — with Gemini

Salam sejahtera dan selamat pagi, rakan-rakan sekalian! 👋 The email arrives: "Revise and resubmit." You open the file and there it is — three reviewers, forty-one comments, and Reviewer 2 in a mood. 😅 The science is the easy part; the hard part is replying to every point clearly, tracking each change, and staying gracious when you'd rather argue. This week we let Gemini turn that wall of comments into a tidy point-by-point response table — conceding where the reviewer's right, pushing back politely where they're not. You keep the scientific judgment; Gemini keeps you organised and diplomatic.

🧪 Prompt Minggu Ini  /  This Week's Prompt

Technique this week: persona + tone-constraint prompting — you set a role (a measured corresponding author) and hard tone rules (concede graciously, disagree politely, never dismissive), then force a complete table. Paste it into Gemini with the reviews.
ROLE: You are a measured, courteous corresponding author responding to peer reviewers — constructive, never defensive.

TASK: From the reviewers' comments and my change-notes below, produce a point-by-point response as a TABLE:
  No | Reviewer comment (paraphrased) | Our response | Change made & location

RULES:
  • Address EVERY comment — skip none.
  • Where the reviewer is right, concede graciously and state the fix.
  • Where you disagree, push back politely with reasoning — never dismissive.
  • Cite the section/line where each change appears.
  • Thank the reviewers once at the top, not in every row.
  • Flag any comment you can't answer without more input from me.

REVIEWERS' COMMENTS: [paste comments]
MY NOTES / WHAT I CHANGED: [paste your notes]

🔒 Selamatkah Data Saya?  /  Is My Data Safe?

Short version: the account you sign in with decides your protection — not the tool.

✅ GOOD NEWS — you're covered. FTSM's faculty Google AI Pro is Workspace-managed, so uploads aren't used to train models, aren't seen by human reviewers, and stay inside UKM's domain.
⚠️ ONE CATCH — only while signed into UKM. The protection travels with the login, not the laptop. If anyone slips into a personal Google account (even a paid "Pro" one) for student work, it's gone. Keep student docs on the UKM login.
👍 HABIT — skip the 👍/👎 on real student documents. Rating a response is the one action that can send that content for human review. Just don't tap it.

🌍 For the sovereignty-minded: "not trained on" isn't the same as "stored in Malaysia." True data-residency is a region setting your IT admin controls — worth a quick check if that's a hard requirement.
Penafian / Disclaimers. AI output is assistive, not authoritative — verify every point against the source and your own expertise. Protect student privacy: only upload work where UKM's data-protection and academic-integrity policies allow it, and get consent where required. Disclose AI use per faculty guidelines. Technique to end on: one-shot web-page generation — describe the page you want and get a complete, single-file site back. Paste this into Gemini.
BUILD ME A SINGLE-FILE WEBPAGE (one .html file, no external files) that is [a resource hub for my course / lab / research group].

INCLUDE:
  • A clear title and a one-line intro.
  • Sections for [topics / weeks / resources], each with a short description and links.
  • A simple top navigation, and a clean, readable, mobile-friendly layout in a [colour] theme.
RULES: inline CSS only, no external libraries. Give me the complete HTML in one block I can save as index.html and open in a browser.
